📌 Must Know Before You Go
  1. Start Lila every 2–3 days when parked. Otherwise the engine battery drains and she won't start.
  2. Gray water tank is smaller than fresh (22 vs 33 gal). Dump gray every time you refill fresh.
  3. Fresair (swamp cooler): only use the water feature when it's truly hot, and always turn the water OFF before turning the unit off.
  4. Close the roof fan hatch before driving. Highway wind will break it.
  5. Turn off lights and water when you leave Lila. Power conservation is the name of the game.
  6. Empty the fruit basket before driving on dirt roads. Otherwise you'll be making smoothies you didn't ask for.
  7. Solar needs direct sun. If you're relying on solar to charge, park where sunlight actually hits the panels.
  8. Lila is NOT 4WD. Do not drive on 4WD-only roads or in sand. She will get stuck.

Now let's get you on the road.

✅ Getting Started

The first 10 minutes. Don't stress.

Here's all you really need to do before driving off:

  1. Adjust the driver's seat and mirrors
  2. Make sure these switches are OFF until you need them: hot water tank, water pump, fresh-air cooler water function (more on these below)
  3. Take a quick peek at the battery level on the control panel. Should be 80%+ at pickup
  4. Take a deep breath. You're good. Go.

To make the table:

  • Roll up the foam mattress topper, toss it on the front passenger seat
  • The two wide cushions push back against the wall (they're the base)
  • The two skinny cushions sit on top of the wide ones
  • Grab the two table poles + tabletop from under the bed
  • Remember the magic move for ALL pole adjustments: pull out, push down, push in, pull up. That's how you loosen and tighten everything.

To make the bed:

  • Just reverse it. Loosen the table top, remove poles, lay the tabletop down (put the extender piece on first, inside), pull cushions forward, drop the foam topper on top.
  • Tuck the cushions snug so you don't slip into the cracks at 3am.
💡 Tip

There's a storage compartment in the back with a handle that lifts up. Great for stashing shoes, books, or snacks.

⚠️ Heads Up

The table itself isn't built to hold a person's weight. Use it for plates, laptops, and books, but don't sit, kneel, or step on it.

💡Power (a.k.a. The Most Important Thing)

If you're new to van life, here's the golden rule: conserve power. Lila has plenty for normal living, but only if you're intentional.

The control panel ("the motherboard") lives in the first cabinet on the left above the bed. It tells you:

  • Battery %: keep an eye on this. Above 50% = chill. Below 30% = time to drive (which charges it) or hook up to shore power.
  • Fresh water tank %: a full tank gets you roughly 4–7 days with limited showers and conservative dishwashing
  • AC loads / PV charger / DC system: detailed power readouts. Just don't change the AC current limit (set to 15) or the Pump Auto setting.

Power-saving habits:

  • Turn off the hot water tank when you're not using it (it's a power hog)
  • Turn off the water pump when not in use
  • Same for the fresh-air cooler's water function
  • Unplug things you're not actively using

Shore power: If you've got access to an outlet (campground, friend's driveway), the shore cable is stored under the cushion on the left side, where the main power panel lives. Just plug in and you're charging.

⚠️ Heads Up — Posting Up Somewhere Remote?

If you're parked for more than a few days without driving, start Lila up and let her run for 10–15 minutes every 3 days. This keeps the chassis (engine) battery from draining. The house batteries get charged by solar and shore power, but the engine battery only charges when she's running. Skip this and you may find a van that won't start when it's time to leave.

🚿Water + Hot Water

Cold water is easy. Flip the water pump switch (under the fruit basket on the wall) and use the sink like normal.

Hot water takes 3 steps and about 10–15 minutes to warm up:

  1. Lift the cushion on the left side of the bench seat. Flip the "tank water" switch UP. It'll glow red when on.
  2. Flip the water pump switch ON (under the fruit basket).
  3. Flip the hot water switch ON (also under the fruit basket).

Wait about 10 minutes. You're ready.

💡 Tip

The water temperature dial is on the hot water tank itself, under the sink. I've set it to a good spot. Feel free to nudge if needed.

⚠️ Heads Up

Always turn the hot water tank back OFF when you're done. It uses a lot of power and you don't want it running overnight.

🚽The Toilet

Lila has a portable freshwater-flush toilet. The top half holds clean water for flushing. The bottom half holds the waste. They lock together with side latches. Simple to use, simple to empty.

The parts you'll touch:

  • Round button on top = the flush pump. Press to spray water into the bowl.
  • PULL handle on the front = the trap door between the bowl and the waste tank. Pull it open to drain, push it closed to seal.
  • Small round cap on top = freshwater fill spout.
  • Large nozzle/cap on the back = where you pour out the waste when emptying.
  • Level indicator on the waste tank = shows how full the bottom tank is. Glance at this before each use so you know when it's dump time.

Daily use:

  • Lift the lid
  • Do your thing
  • Press the flush button on top to spray water in
  • For #2: pull the PULL handle to open the trap, then flush
  • Always push the PULL handle closed when you're done. This seals the bowl and keeps odors down.
💡 Tip

For #1, you can leave the trap closed and just do a quick flush. For #2, open the trap, flush, then close it back up.

⚠️ Toilet Paper Rule — No Exceptions

NO toilet paper goes in the toilet. Ever. All TP goes in the lined trash bin next to the toilet. This keeps the dump process clean, prevents clogs, and keeps the bottom tank from getting nasty.

When to empty: Glance at the level indicator on the waste tank. When it's about 3/4 full, plan a dump stop. Don't push past full or it gets messy.

How to empty:

  1. Unlatch the top from the bottom (latches on the sides)
  2. Carry the bottom tank by the handle to a dump station or designated waste site
  3. Pull out the nozzle on the back and unscrew it
  4. Tilt and pour. Press the small air vent button on top of the spout as you pour (this controls flow and prevents splash-back)
  5. Rinse the tank with water, dump the rinse too
  6. Reattach the top, latch it down

Refilling fresh water (top tank):

  • Unscrew the small round cap on top
  • Fill from the sink or any potable source
  • Screw the cap back on

🌊Gray Water

Lila has a 22-gallon gray water tank that catches water from the sink and the shower. There's no black water tank. Your toilet waste lives in the portable toilet (see above), which you empty separately.

⚠️ Heads Up — No Gray Tank Gauge

There's no level indicator for the gray water tank, so you can't check how full it is. The only signal you'll get is a sink that suddenly won't drain. Two things to remember:

  • Your gray tank (22 gal) is smaller than your fresh tank (33 gal). It fills up before your fresh runs out.
  • A good rule of thumb: dump gray every time you refill fresh, even if you "feel like" you have room. You don't.

How to dump:

  1. Grab the gray water hose from the garage (same area as the fresh water hose and filter)
  2. Take the end with the flat black nozzle underneath the van and connect it to the gray water outlet (look for the matching grooves and twist to lock)
  3. Pull the lever to release the drain plug
  4. Place the other end of the hose into the dump station hole
  5. Let it drain fully
  6. Rinse the hose with water before rolling it back up
  7. Make sure all the water is out of the hose before storing. Otherwise it'll get funky in there.

⚠️ Heads Up — Starlink Needs Open Sky

Starlink is satellite internet, which means it needs a clear view of the sky to work. If you're parked under thick tree cover, in a deep canyon, against a tall cliff, or inside any kind of structure, the signal will drop or fail entirely.

If your internet isn't working, look up. If you can't see open sky, move the van.

💡 Tip for Remote Workers

If you need reliable internet for work, scout your overnight spot during daylight with the sky in mind. Open meadows, parking lots, beaches, and clearings are great. Dense forest is beautiful but offline.

🛣️ Life on the Road

A few things that'll make your trip smoother.

Parking:

  • Lila stands 9'2" tall (110 inches). She does NOT fit in standard parking garages (most clear at 6'8"–8'). Always park in surface lots, and watch for drive-thrus, low overhangs, and tree branches.
  • For overnights: campgrounds, BLM land, dispersed camping spots, and apps like iOverlander, FreeRoam, and The Dyrt are your best friends.
⚠️ Heads Up — Lila Is NOT 4WD

Lila is a Ram Promaster 2500, which is front-wheel-drive only. Do not take her on 4WD-only roads, and do not drive in sand. She will get stuck, and tow bills out in the wilderness are eye-watering. Stick to paved roads and well-maintained dirt roads. If a road feels questionable, turn around. The view at the next spot will be just as good.

🌿 Dispersed Camping (free camping on public land)
  • Stay 200ft from water sources
  • Pack out everything you bring in (yes, even orange peels)
  • Use existing fire rings only, and check fire restrictions before lighting anything
  • Be quiet, be kind, leave it better than you found it 🌲
🌿 Leave No Trace

Lila travels through some sacred landscapes. Treat them like the temples they are. That means no trash, no soap in streams, no off-trail driving, no playing music out loud at campsites.

Driving tips:

  • Take turns slow. Lila is taller and a bit top-heavy
  • Use lower gears on big descents
  • Wind affects her more than a car. White-knuckle bridges happen. You're fine.
⛽ About Lila's Gas
  • Lila takes regular unleaded (87 octane). No premium needed.
  • Expect roughly 14–17 mpg. Plan fill-ups around that, especially in remote stretches.
  • The heater also pulls from this same gas tank. So if you're running low on fuel and using the heater overnight, you're double-dipping. Don't get caught with an empty tank in the cold.

Safety basics:

  • Trust your gut on parking spots. If it feels off, it probably is. Drive 5 more minutes.
  • Lock the doors when you sleep. I use the key fob to set the alarm.
  • For emergencies, text or call: 757-777-6924

🤔 What to Know Before You Go

Common things renters forget:

  • Closing the roof vent before driving. If it's open, close it. (It can break in highway wind.)
  • Securing loose items. That coffee mug will become a projectile. Stow before you go.
  • Turning OFF water pump + hot water before bed or before driving.
  • Pushing the toilet PULL handle closed between uses (keeps odors down).
  • Putting toilet paper in the toilet. Don't! It always goes in the trash.
  • Window covers stored properly (not loose on the bed)
  • Topping off water before heading remote. There's no faucet in the desert.
  • Dumping gray water on a schedule. No gauge to check, so dump every time you refill fresh.
  • Starting Lila every 3 days if you're posted up. (See the Power section.)

📋 House Rules

Just a couple of essentials so the next renter walks into a fresh, ready Lila too.

🧹 Returning Lila

We'll keep this stress-free. Just leave her in roughly the shape you found her.

Before you return:

  • Take out trash
  • Empty and rinse the toilet's bottom tank at a dump station or flushable toilet
  • Wipe down counters + table
  • Sweep / shake out crumbs
  • Empty the fridge of your food
  • Turn OFF: hot water tank, water pump, fresh-air cooler, heater
  • Make sure windows are closed and covers are on or stored flat
  • Return the shore cable to its spot under left storage
  • Top off the gas tank
  • Empty the gray water tank at a dump station
  • Rinse and fully drain the gray water hose before storing

That's it. No hidden checklist, no fees for normal wear and tear. Just leave her ready for the next adventurer.
